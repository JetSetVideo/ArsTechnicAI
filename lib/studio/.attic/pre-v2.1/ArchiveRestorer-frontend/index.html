Key frames

Pin the current sliders to the frame you are looking at. Between two key frames the values are interpolated, so a shot that drifts is treated by a grade that drifts with it.

Layers masked adjustments, applied in order

Damage & Missing Pixels

Emulsion loss, tears and perforation damage destroy areas too large to inpaint honestly from within one frame. This finds them by temporal singularity — real content persists across neighbours, damage does not — then transplants what a neighbouring frame actually photographed, regrained and level-matched so the repair is not a clean patch on grainy film.

Reframe & Outpaint

Much of this reel is framed too tightly, and levelling voids the corners besides. Pulling back declares the composition you meant; the margin it exposes is answered first from what neighbouring frames already photographed, and only then, if you ask, by the image model.

Aspect stays locked to the frame. Drag the gold rectangle on the picture to pull back and recrop; the shaded area is what the film never covered.

Colour & Gradation

Measures the section in front of you, including whether it drifts from its own start to its own end — a shot that fades cannot be fixed by a single static grade.

Match against a reference section

Subject Isolation

Subjects are found either by the vision model, which can name them, or locally from faces, bodies, motion and saliency, which numbers them but needs no credential. Either way GrabCut turns each box into a pixel matte here on this machine. Attach a matte to a layer to treat that subject on its own.

Stored mattes

Missing Frames

Rebuilds destroyed frames from their neighbours with motion-compensated interpolation. A frame is only offered once it stands out from its own shot and the repair has been rehearsed successfully — a soft frame in a soft shot is left alone, because interpolating it only makes it softer.
